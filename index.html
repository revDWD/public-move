How it works

As easy as rideshare. Run like transit.

Riders get the convenience of an app. The city gets one coordinated fleet that it owns, measures and improves in public.

  1. 1

    Request

    Pick a destination. The app offers every vehicle that fits your trip and your access needs, with the full fare and subsidy shown up front.

  2. 2

    Ride

    The nearest vehicle comes to you, or to the closest corner or corridor stop. Unlock with a code, follow the route live, and yield automatically to emergencies.

  3. 3

    Return

    Every outbound trip reserves a way back. Fares stop at $18 a month, and work, healthcare and school trips are covered.

The fleet

Six vehicle types, one network.

All electric and all publicly owned, from two-seat pods to corridor buses. Booking assigns the closest vehicle that fits.

Public benefit

Practical outcomes for riders and cities.

Lower transportation costs

Fares cover energy and upkeep at cost, a public subsidy halves them, and the monthly cap is $18. A household that rides instead of owning a car can save around $13,000 a year.

APTA, public transportation savings estimates

More people can get around

Door-to-door service for people who don’t drive: older adults, teens, shift workers and anyone without a car.

Coverage beyond the routes

Pods and shuttles reach the blocks that fixed routes miss, and feed riders into high-capacity corridors and stations.

Less congestion and parking

Shared fleets can do the work of many more private cars, freeing street space.

ITF/OECD Lisbon study, modelled scenario

Cleaner air

An all-electric fleet, charged at depots, with shared trips that cut emissions per rider.

Accessibility built in

Ramp vehicles within 8 minutes in every neighbourhood, step-free routing, quiet cabins and spoken updates.

Accountable public ownership

Pickup times, costs and incidents are published. No surge pricing, tips, ads, premium tiers or paid queue-jumping.

The service guarantee

A ride when you need one, published every day.

Maximum pickup times by area, 24/7 service for essential travel, and a guaranteed way home. When a promise is missed, the trip is free and the delay is logged publicly.

For cities and transit agencies

An operating model you can pilot.

Public / Move extends existing transit rather than replacing it. Start with one district and a few corridors, measure everything, then grow.

01

Publicly owned fleet

The agency owns or leases vehicles and depots and sets service standards. Operations and remote assistance can be contracted, with performance clauses.

02

Feeds the trunk lines

Pods and shuttles connect riders to rail and bus hubs. Corridor buses run where demand is densest.

03

At-cost fares, clear subsidy

Every receipt shows energy, upkeep and accessible service, the public subsidy and any benefit applied.

04

Open performance data

Pickup times, coverage, accessibility response and safety reporting published daily by district.
